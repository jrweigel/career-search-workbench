## Purpose

Use this file to keep the candidate narrative consistent across all artifacts and responses.

## Leadership Messaging Baseline

Apply these rules across resume, LinkedIn, pitch, outreach, thank-you notes, and application answers.

1. State a clear target objective. Leaders signal intent.
2. Lead with outcomes and business impact. Leaders are measured by results.
3. Be concise and skimmable. Leaders communicate with precision.
4. Reduce reviewer risk by removing ambiguity. If the reader must guess, trust drops.
5. Use action ownership language. Describe what you drove, built, led, or owned.
6. Show evidence instead of self-praise. Replace adjective stacks with proof.
7. Use STAR logic in written form: context, action, result.
8. Quantify wherever possible. Percent, dollars, time, scale, frequency, and scope all count.
9. Preserve truth. Strong framing is good. Fabrication is disqualifying.
10. Tailor for the role while preserving the same core identity.

## Canon Structure

Capture and maintain five elements.

1. Core thesis: the concise statement of who the candidate is and what they are known for.
2. Earned secret: the unique operational insight earned through lived experience.
3. Value creation pattern: how the candidate repeatedly creates impact.
4. Signature strengths: two to three repeatable strengths that appear in stories.
5. Preferred problem set: the types of problems the candidate should target.

## Consistency Rules

1. Every major artifact should reinforce the same core thesis.
2. Resume bullets should show proof of the value creation pattern.
3. LinkedIn About should narrate the thesis and earned secret clearly.
4. Outreach messages should open with a thesis aligned hook, not generic intent.
5. Interview answers should connect local examples back to the same broader identity.
6. The objective should be explicit in resume summary, LinkedIn About above the fold, and core pitch variants.
7. Claims should be specific enough to be verifiable and low-risk for the reader to trust.
8. Acronyms, internal project names, and team-specific shorthand should be translated for external readers.

## Surface Rules

### Resume

1. Prioritize outcome bullets over responsibility bullets.
2. Keep sections scan-first with whitespace and clear hierarchy.
3. Use selective emphasis for hooks only. Over-highlighting reduces effect.
4. Keep length calibrated by stage: one page early career, two pages for most experienced candidates.
5. Include links in digital versions when they validate meaningful claims.

### LinkedIn

1. Preserve the same objective and proof spine used in resume bullets.
2. Optimize above-fold text to earn the click on "see more."
3. During active search, keep About role-forward and objective-forward.
4. During network mode, expand into aspirations, interests, and point of view without diluting target identity.
5. Use Featured and supporting sections to add credibility depth, not clutter.
6. Prefer links that verify your work and reinforce your expertise.

### Other Personal Branding Surfaces

1. `pitch`: Convert thesis to concise spoken variants with concrete proof.
2. `outreach`: Use role-aligned hooks, specific asks, and one proof signal per message.
3. `thankyou`: Reinforce fit with one concrete callback from the conversation.
4. `apply`: Keep written answers consistent with thesis and measurable evidence.

## Drift Checks

Mark drift when any of these happen.

1. Different target identities appear across resume, LinkedIn, and pitch.
2. Stories prove work that does not match stated target role.
3. Language is polished but unsupported by evidence.
4. The candidate sounds functional in one surface and strategic in another.
5. Objective or target role shifts between surfaces without an intentional reason.
6. Evidence quality differs sharply between channels, creating credibility gaps.

## Command Usage

Use this canon in these workflows.

1. pitch: draft and refine the thesis and variants.
2. resume: align bullets and summary to the canon.
3. linkedin: align headline, About, experience, and featured proof.
4. outreach and thankyou: keep message framing consistent.
5. apply: keep application answers aligned with the same objective and evidence style.
6. stories: test whether top stories reinforce the same identity.

## Update Triggers

Update canon elements after:

1. New positioning statement created or revised.
2. New high signal story added.
3. Material change in target role, level, or industry.
4. Repeated feedback indicating narrative confusion.
